The format

Three Days. One Real Product.

Find a problem worth solving. Form a team of 3–5, build a working MVP, document at least one meaningful iteration, and launch your product.

DEFINE

Competition rules & recognition

Build It. Improve It. Show It.

We value useful products and thoughtful decisions, not unnecessary technical complexity.

Teams & Eligibility

3–5 students per team. UIUC undergraduate and master's students from all majors are welcome. Join with a team or find one Friday evening.

Original Weekend Work

Build your competition MVP during the weekend. Existing libraries, APIs, frameworks and AI assistants are allowed, but prebuilt products cannot be entered as finished work.

Iteration Is Mandatory

Show at least one documented product improvement informed by feedback or testing. Without it, a team is ineligible for awards.

How Judging Works

Judges score independently. A team's final score is the average of its judge scores; all eligible teams are evaluated using the same rubric. Final rubric and tie-break rules will be shared before the event.

Problem & User Understanding25%

Product Value & Potential Impact25%

Working MVP & Execution25%

Iteration & Product Decisions15%

Presentation10%

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to know how to code?

No. All majors are welcome. Teams need a practical way to build and demonstrate their MVP.

Do I need a team or project idea ahead of time?

No. Friday team formation and a Real Problem Bank will help you find both teammates and a problem worth solving.

Can I use AI tools or existing code libraries?

Yes, tools and libraries are welcome. Your competition MVP must be built during the event weekend; you cannot enter a previously completed product.

What counts as an iteration?

Make and document a meaningful change to your MVP based on user, mentor or testing feedback. Teams without evidence of iteration cannot win an award.

How many mentor sessions are required?

At least two two-hour Saturday mentor session blocks per team, with a report of feedback and actions taken.

Will winning projects be deployed by the university?

No adoption is guaranteed. Organizers aim to help promising projects explore follow-up development and connect with stakeholders where possible.

Where is the event?

The current tentative location is the iSchool Hub, fourth floor, pending confirmation.

Where can I ask questions?

Contact Dr. Yun Huang at yunhuang@illinois.edu.
